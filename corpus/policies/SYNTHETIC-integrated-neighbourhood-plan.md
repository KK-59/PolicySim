# Brackenmoor Integrated Neighbourhood Delivery Plan 2026/27

**Brackenmoor Place-Based Partnership** · Draft for board approval · September 2026

## 1. Purpose

Brackenmoor serves a registered population of approximately 50,000 people across one primary care
network, one community provider and a single acute site. Demand has been broadly flat for three
years while waits have lengthened, which indicates a flow problem rather than a demand problem.

This plan sets out six operational commitments for 2026/27. Each is expressed as a measurable
change against the current baseline so that delivery can be tracked quarterly.

## 2. Commitments

### 2.1 Community capacity

The binding constraint on the neighbourhood is community home-visit capacity, currently **four
visits per day**. We will **double community home-visit capacity to eight visits per day** by the
end of Q2, through a second community nursing round staffed by the existing establishment working
a revised rota.

### 2.2 Primary care access

We will fund **two additional GP sessions per day** across the network, over and above the six
sessions currently delivered. On the current pattern of fifteen usable slots per session, this
represents approximately thirty additional appointments per working day.

### 2.3 Channel mix

One third of follow-up appointments are currently conducted by telephone. We will increase the
**share of follow-up appointments delivered by telephone to 50 per cent**, prioritising routine
review appointments where physical examination is not clinically indicated. In-person capacity
released by this change is to be redirected to first appointments, not absorbed.

### 2.4 Remote monitoring

We will **triple the intensity of remote monitoring** for patients discharged with long-term
conditions, expanding from the current cohort to all patients discharged following an admission of
three days or longer. Monitoring is a supplement to review, not a replacement for it.

### 2.5 Discharge pathways

Too many discharges default to an outpatient follow-up that the patient must travel to. We will
route **40 per cent of hospital discharges to community follow-up** rather than outpatient
clinics, where the clinical need can be met at home.

### 2.6 Discharge timing

Weekend discharge activity is negligible, which concentrates pressure on Monday. We will move to
seven-day discharge working, reducing the **share of discharges timed to weekdays to 75 per cent**.

## 3. What this plan does not claim

The partnership notes three limits on the above. First, released capacity may be refilled by
previously unmet need. Second, relieving the community constraint may relocate the bottleneck to
primary care rather than clearing it. Third, none of these commitments addresses workforce supply,
which remains the principal medium-term risk.

## 4. Measurement

Delivery will be assessed against the 2025/26 baseline at each quarter end. The partnership expects
the median wait to improve before the ninetieth percentile does, and will report both rather than
the median alone.
